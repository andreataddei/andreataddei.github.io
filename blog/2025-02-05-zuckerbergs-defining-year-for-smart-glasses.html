Zuckerberg's Defining Year for Smart Glasses

05 Feb 2025 Source: MIT Technology Review XR · AI

On an earnings call weeks into the year, Mark Zuckerberg called 2025 a "defining year" for smart glasses. The subtext: the hardware finally exists, and the only missing ingredient is an AI experience good enough to make people wear a computer on their face.

The Ray-Ban Meta glasses had already proven the demand side — millions of units sold to people who were not early adopters, buying them for photos and music rather than any sci-fi promise. That is the rarest thing in XR: a product people wear because they like it.

What 2025 had to decide is what the glasses are for. Voice assistants were the first answer, and multimodal AI made them dramatically better: ask what you are looking at, get a real answer. The next steps were display — putting information in the world instead of on a phone screen.

The engineering challenge is unforgiving. Every gram on the face is noticed; every milliwatt drains a battery the size of a temple arm. The product only works if optics, silicon and software converge on something indistinguishable from normal glasses.

MIT Technology Review's framing was right: this is not a hardware race but a race to prove the use case. If AI on the face feels indispensable, glasses win. If it feels like a demo, the category stalls again.

The earnings-call framing also told the industry where the money will flow: Meta's wearables spending for the year leaned overwhelmingly toward glasses and AI rather than the metaverse rhetoric of the years before. Roadmaps follow capital, and in XR the capital was walking toward the face.
