Project Moohan: The First Android XR Headset

04 Mar 2025 Source: Sammy Fans XR · AI

At Mobile World Congress, Samsung revealed Project Moohan, the first headset running Android XR. After months of renders and rumors, visitors could finally put Google's spatial operating system on their heads.

The strategic weight exceeds the hardware. Android XR is Google's attempt to do for headsets what Android did for phones: own the platform layer while many manufacturers compete on devices. Gemini is woven through the experience — not as an app, but as the primary interface to the system.

The playbook is visible. Headsets with passthrough cameras and hand tracking are the developer phase, where Google ships the SDKs and courts creators. The consumer phase arrives when that ecosystem runs on glasses light enough to forget you are wearing them.

Against that, Apple's Vision platform is a vertical stack: superb hardware, closed ecosystem, premium price. Samsung and Google's answer is horizontal — an OS any OEM can license, a silicon partner in Qualcomm, and an install base that boots up the moment existing Android developers port over.

For developers, 2025 was the year spatial computing stopped meaning "learn a proprietary SDK." Android tooling, ML runtimes and 3D engines already cover most of the gap. The platform war has formally opened.

MWC was the right stage for the reveal: a mobile-industry audience, mobile-industry cadence, and mobile-industry expectations about price tiers and yearly refreshes. The message beneath the demo was that XR is graduating from an exotic category into the device roadmaps of every phone maker on the floor. For Samsung the stakes extend past one product: its display and silicon divisions supply half the industry, and a healthy spatial market grows the very components it sells.
