Loomos AI Glasses Take Aim at the Ray-Bans

20 Feb 2025 Source: Forbes XR · AI

The clearest sign a product category is real is when challengers start arriving. Loomos shipped AI glasses aimed squarely at Ray-Ban Meta: camera, open-ear audio, an assistant and a price tag positioned to undercut the incumbent.

The interesting engineering choice is what these startups put in and leave out. Loomos and its peers skip display optics — the hardest, heaviest, most expensive subsystem — and concentrate the budget on camera quality, battery life and speakers. The assistant, not the screen, is the interface.

That mirrors how the smartphone matured: the first movers proved demand, the fast followers optimized the formula. Ray-Ban Meta demonstrated that people will wear AI glasses; the 2025 wave tested how cheap and how good they could get.

There is a platform angle too. These devices are largely assistant-agnostic — they can plug into whichever LLM performs best. That makes them beneficiaries of the AI race rather than combatants in it.

For the consumer, competition here means better batteries, better mics and falling prices. For the industry, it means the face is becoming contested ground — with Meta holding the high ground and everyone else attacking the price ladder beneath it.

Forbes' review cycle captured the deeper shift: this is no longer a race between ecosystems, but between form factors — and each new entrant proves the demand curve Meta drew is real, repeatable, and not tied to one brand's distribution deal with one eyewear giant. The sub-$300 price band these challengers target is also where unit economics finally work: it is the tier where glasses stop being a gadget purchase and become an impulse buy at the optician.
