ESA and Telespazio Rewire Space Ops with AI

14 Apr 2025 Source: Orbital Today Space · AI

The least visible part of the space industry is the part keeping everything alive: operations. In April, ESA and Telespazio announced a collaboration to bring machine intelligence into spacecraft operations themselves, with the upcoming CO2M carbon-monitoring mission as the demonstration platform.

Flying a satellite is a 24/7 discipline of telemetry monitoring, maneuver planning and anomaly response, historically staffed by console teams watching dashboards. As constellations scale from tens to hundreds of spacecraft, that model breaks — no operations center can scale linearly with the fleet.

AI operations attack the problem at three levels: anomaly detection that learns each spacecraft's normal and flags deviations in seconds, automated scheduling that turns payload and maneuver planning into an optimization problem, and assistants that let one engineer safely supervise many vehicles.

CO2M is a fitting testbed. Europe's carbon-monitoring mission will fly a new generation of hyperspectral instruments producing continuous data streams — exactly the workload that overwhelms manual procedures.

The sector nickname for this discipline is already forming: MLOps, transplanted to orbit. The skills it needs — telemetry pipelines, time-series models, decision automation under uncertainty — are ordinary ML engineering skills in an extraordinary setting. Expect operations AI to become as standard a spacecraft subsystem as a star tracker.

The collaboration also points at a talent pipeline: Telespazio's operators and ESA's flight dynamics teams are being asked to think like ML engineers, and the contractors who can speak both languages — spacecraft constraints and modern data pipelines — are becoming the scarce profile in European space hiring.
