Sentinel-1D Opens Its Data to the World

20 Apr 2026 Source: Sentinel Online EO · Copernicus

Commissioning is over, the numbers are in, and the door is open: Sentinel-1D user data opened on 17 April 2026. The new radar satellite's SAR products are calibrated, their quality confirmed to be in line with Sentinel-1 data specifications — which means downstream services can absorb the new unit without re-engineering anything.

The operational choreography is worth appreciating. ESA planned a three-satellite constellation from mid-April to mid-June, enabling a smooth integration of Sentinel-1D into the pipeline while Sentinel-1A, the veteran of the family, still held the line. Continuity in EO is not a state; it is a relay race, and handovers are where records get dropped.

Sentinel-1's C-band radar sees through clouds, smoke and darkness, which is why its products anchor services that cannot afford to wait for daylight or clear skies: flood extent in monsoon season, ground deformation before an earthquake aftershock sequence, vessel detection in illegal fishing zones, ice charts for polar shipping. Every one of those services depends on two boring properties: calibrated data, and a stable interface that does not change under them. The commissioning team's job is to make a new spacecraft boring as fast as possible.

The timing was not idle. As June would confirm, Sentinel-1A — the satellite that carried the mission alone after the loss of 1B in 2021 — was nearing the end of its operational life. Opening 1D's data in April meant the relay baton passed cleanly, with overlap instead of a gap.

For data users the announcement reads as boilerplate; for anyone who runs operational monitoring it is the sound of the floor being reinforced. The archive grows unbroken, the algorithms keep working, and Europe's all-weather eyes stay open.
