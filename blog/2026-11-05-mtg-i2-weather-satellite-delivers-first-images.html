MTG-I2 Weather Satellite Delivers First Images

05 Nov 2026 Source: ESA EO · Space

Europe's newest weather eye has opened. MTG-I2, the second imager of the Meteosat Third Generation mission, has delivered its first images of weather systems sweeping over Europe and Africa — fog pooling over central Europe, deep convection boiling over the Mediterranean, a wildfire smoke plume over Portugal, all captured in a single 45-hour sequence from 3 November.

The headline number is the repeat cycle. Once fully operational, MTG-I2's Flexible Combined Imager will run in rapid scan mode, imaging Europe and North Africa every 2.5 minutes. Its sibling, the first MTG imager, covers a full hemisphere every 10 minutes. Together they turn Europe's geostationary watch from a slideshow into something close to film.

The geometry is the quiet enabler. From 36,000 kilometers above the same point on the equator — the "hawk's eye" position — the satellite never changes perspective, so every 2.5-minute frame is directly comparable with the last. That is what makes rapid evolution visible: forecasters watch a storm cell grow frame by frame rather than infer its behavior between passes.

The engineering chain behind a single frame is worth pausing on: ESA developed the satellite through Thales Alenia Space against EUMETSAT's operational requirements, and it launched on 27 August 2026 on an Ariane 6, completing the first family of three MTG spacecraft.

Calibration continues until operations begin in mid-2027, with a second satellite family already planned to carry the mission past 2040. The faster Europe scans its sky, the earlier severe weather gets flagged — and the 2.5-minute era starts now.
