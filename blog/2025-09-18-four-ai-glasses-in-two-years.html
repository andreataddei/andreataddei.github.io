Four AI Glasses in Two Years

18 Sep 2025 Source: The Verge XR · AI

Zoom out from any single announcement and the velocity is the story. Meta went from one smart-glasses model in 2023 to four distinct lines by late 2025 — audio-first, camera, sport, and display — each aimed at a different slice of the same bet: the face is the next platform.

Four product lines in two years is consumer-electronics pace, not XR pace. The glasses inherited the development rhythm of earbuds and watches: annual refreshes, price tiers, celebrity designer variants. The industry that spent a decade on concept videos now ships on a supply-chain cadence.

The compounding happens on the data side too. Every camera frame spoken query and gesture feeds the loop that makes the next assistant better — a moat that no headset developer enjoys, because glasses are worn outdoors, all day, in the messiness of real life.

The competitive read: Apple holds the premium tier with Vision Pro but has no glasses; Google's Android XR announced its entry for the following year. Meta's response was to occupy every tier before the platform war formally opens.

The takeaway for builders is straightforward. When a category ships at this cadence, the scarce thing stops being hardware and becomes experiences — translation, navigation, memory, live captions — that make a worn computer feel indispensable. Four glasses lines mean Meta believes; the applications are still anyone's to write.

The Verge's roundup also caught the detail that matters most to builders: Meta simultaneously opened the glasses platform's developer surface — multimodal AI APIs, gesture input from the band — meaning third parties can now write for the face, not just watch Meta occupy it.
