AI Onboard: The Space Cloud Era Begins

5 Nov 2026 Source: CorriereComunicazioni Space · AI · EO

In traditional Earth observation systems, sensors collect large volumes of data that are sent to the ground for processing. That architecture is now being inverted: Space Cloud platforms and intelligent constellations integrate high-performance computing and data fusion in orbit, sharing compute and observations, orchestrating resources, and improving tracking and response.

The pieces are arriving almost simultaneously. Onboard models detect fires and ships at the sensor, as Planet demonstrated in the spring and Telepix's autonomous geolocation confirmed in the fall. Radiation-tolerant AI accelerators are becoming catalog parts rather than research projects. And the economics have flipped: when a constellation images the entire planet daily, the downlink is the bottleneck — so moving interpretation before transmission stops being exotic and becomes the obvious design.

The Space Cloud framing adds a second-order idea: not just one smart satellite, but constellations that share computation and fuse observations among themselves — cross-cueing, where one spacecraft's detection tasks another's high-resolution sensor, and results that never need to touch the ground until they matter.

The open challenges are equally concrete, and the industry is honest about them. Thermal management for sustained orbital compute, radiation effects on large model weights, verification of autonomous systems you cannot physically reach, and the security of an orbital edge that becomes a target like any other infrastructure. Each is an engineering frontier; none is a wall.

What follows from here is the pattern that defined this whole year, made physical: intelligence moving to where the data is born. First the archive moved to the cloud; now the model is moving to the sensor. The satellite is becoming a computer with a planet as its peripheral — and the teams that learn to program it well are just getting started.
