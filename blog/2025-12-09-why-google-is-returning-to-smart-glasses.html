Why Google Is Returning to Smart Glasses

09 Dec 2025 Source: Los Angeles Times XR · AI

No company has more scar tissue in this category. Google announced its return to smart glasses a dozen years after Google Glass — and the differences between the two attempts read like a case study in what changed.

The first failure was social, not technical: a face-mounted camera in 2013 arrived before the world had norms for it, and the prototype's cyborg aesthetics made its wearer the interface problem. Glass taught the industry that eyewear is a fashion product judged by everyone around you, not just the person wearing it.

The second failure was the missing use case: Glass offered notifications and a camera when the market needed a reason to wear a computer all day. That reason did not exist in 2013. It exists now — a multimodal assistant that sees, hears and answers — because the AI caught up with the hardware.

Hence the 2025 playbook: no visor aesthetics, but frames by Warby Parker and Gentle Monster; no premature display, but audio and camera first; no solo hardware, but a platform licensed to an industry that knows faces.

The historical loop is neat: Google invented the category's hype cycle, exited wounded, watched Meta prove the demand with Ray-Ban, and re-entered when the AI finally justified the hardware. The glasses are back. The difference is that this time, they have a reason to exist.

The LA Times' framing caught the cultural shift too: this time the reaction is anticipation rather than mockery — a decade of AirPods, cameras everywhere and voice assistants normalized the technologies that made Glass's 2013 audience flinch. The product arrived before its culture; the culture has since arrived.
