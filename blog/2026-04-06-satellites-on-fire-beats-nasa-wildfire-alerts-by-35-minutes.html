Satellites on Fire Beats NASA Wildfire Alerts by 35 Minutes

6 Apr 2026 Source: The Next Web EO · AI · Startups

The most instructive satellite story of the spring is a startup that does not own a single satellite. Argentine climate-tech company Satellites on Fire raised $2.7M led by Dalus Capital for an AI platform that detects wildfires 35 minutes ahead of NASA's system — building its detection layer entirely on publicly available data from NASA, NOAA and the European Space Agency.

How do you beat NASA with NASA's own data? By aggregating. The platform pulls imagery from more than eight satellites across agencies, updated as frequently as every five minutes. A single sensor has revisit gaps; a stitched constellation of public feeds has far fewer. The detection advantage comes from covering the seams between satellites — the minutes where a fire grows from a spark into an event and no single instrument happens to be looking.

The engineering moral deserves emphasis because it generalizes far beyond fires. The scarce resource in Earth observation is no longer imagery — it is the layer that fuses many public streams into one continuously updated answer. That layer does not require launch contracts, spectrum licenses or a billion dollars of capital. It requires good data engineering, a model tuned for the failure modes that matter, and customers who need the answer sooner than the incumbents provide it.

Thirty-five minutes sounds abstract until you price it in the units fire services use. A fire caught at ignition size is one crew; a fire caught an hour later is an air fleet. Early detection is the cheapest form of suppression that exists, and detection latency is a software problem — which is why a software company could out-alert the agency that built the sensors.

The hopeful economics: public data plus a narrow, obsessive focus equals planetary-scale protection at startup cost. The satellites were already up there, already paid for, already transmitting. Somebody finally stacked them into a watchdog.
