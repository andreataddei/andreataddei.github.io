Cubesats and the Industrial Uses of Earth Observation

15 Jun 2026 Source: GlobalSpec Space · EO · AI

The most underreported fact about the space industry is its new accessibility. As a GlobalSpec survey of the field notes, some estimates place the design, launch and operation of a small cubesat within the budget of a mid-sized company — a threshold that has quietly moved satellite data capture and communications from "national program" to "line item."

The consequences show up in who is using EO data. The article catalogs industrial uses that previous generations would never have associated with satellites: infrastructure monitoring, logistics, insurance, energy, agriculture. Earth observation data, captured by orbiting sensors, is making inroads into exactly the sectors that once found it too exotic — because AI is now available to do the interpretation that used to require a remote-sensing specialist on staff.

But the piece is honest about the remaining friction, and the friction is instructive. First: measurement differentials. Industrial users must reconcile values from different satellites — and possible sensor miscalibrations — because one mistaken value can snowball through a fragile data pipeline into an expensive wrong decision. Second: integration. EO data competes for attention inside companies that already have sensors, ERPs and analytics stacks; it wins only when it plugs into decisions rather than into a separate dashboard nobody visits.

That second problem — making space data ordinary — is where AI earns its keep. A model that fuses satellite observations with ground truth and internal business data turns "we have imagery" into "tank three is losing volume," which is the only sentence a plant manager cares about.

The hopeful arc is the standardization of the view from above: capabilities once reserved for intelligence agencies becoming workaday industrial infrastructure. Not because the satellites got cheaper alone, but because the whole stack — launch, sensors, models, integration — crossed the threshold where an ordinary company can ask the sky a business question and get a usable answer.
