Europe's launcher gap: ESA warns of a shortage by 2030

12 Aug 2026 Source: RFI Space · Europe

ESA Director-General Josef Aschbacher warned that Europe risks a shortage of homegrown launchers by 2030, just as its flagship constellations come due: the Iris² secure-communications system from 2029, continued Galileo deployment, Copernicus expansion missions, and a wave of national civil and military constellations.

The numbers behind the warning

  • Supply: seven European launches in all of 2025 — four Ariane 6, three Vega-C — while SpaceX alone flew 170, putting roughly 2,500 satellites in orbit.
  • Demand: every European program that produces data — navigation corrections, Earth observation scenes, secure comms — starts with a launch slot that increasingly has to be bought abroad.
  • The gap: Ariane 6 is still ramping cadence and Vega-C only recently returned to flight; production capacity, not rocket design, is the constraint.

For the EO world specifically, the uncomfortable truth is that "where does my data come from?" now has a supply chain answer with a geopolitical shape. A pipeline that treats Sentinel scenes as an infinite tap is betting on someone else's launch schedule.
