Android XR Updates: Galaxy XR Grows Into Everyday Use

23 Mar 2026 Source: Google XR · AI

Google dedicated an entire Android Show to XR, and the message was less about spectacle than about furniture: new updates for the Galaxy XR designed to fit a headset naturally into the things you do every day. The hardware and platforms, as Google put it, are just the canvas — developers are invited to co-create what gets painted on it.

Two features stand out for how unglamorous — and therefore how serious — they are. PC Connect expands your workspace by bringing a real computer into the headset, treating XR as a set of monitors rather than a walled garden of apps. Travel mode stabilizes the viewing experience in motion, solving the nausea problem that has quietly kept headsets out of trains and planes, which is where people actually have idle hours and a reason to want a private 100-inch screen.

The most technically striking item is Likeness, a beta that creates a realistic digital avatar for natural video calls in XR. Presence — feeling like you are actually talking with someone — has been the missing ingredient in remote work. Cartoon avatars are fine for games and off-putting for meetings; photoreal likeness with live expression tracking crosses a psychological threshold that latency charts do not capture.

The forward-looking announcement matters just as much: AI glasses from partners are coming next year, for screen-free assistance and information. That completes the platform architecture — headset for depth, glasses for ubiquity, one Gemini-powered layer of intelligence across both — and it is the clearest sign that XR is being designed as an operating system, not as a peripheral.

The optimistic read is exactly what the enterprise has been waiting for. XR stops being a demo the moment it helps people work, travel and talk. The updates are modest individually; together they describe a platform settling into ordinary life, which is where platforms win.
