Artemis II and a Thousand Observation Satellites

13 Apr 2026 Source: ImpactAlpha Space · EO · AI

The astronauts aboard Artemis II aren't the only ones with a bird's eye view of Earth; more than a thousand observation satellites are circling the planet, and combined with AI that can process their images, they are quietly reorganizing how economic decisions get made. That is the argument of a detailed ImpactAlpha survey of the real-time Earth monitoring market — and the numbers back it up.

"The number of satellites is exploding. The use cases are exploding," as one climate investor quoted in the piece puts it. From an investor's perspective, the sector has crossed from speculative to infrastructural: monitoring is no longer a science program, it is a supply-chain input. Ports counting ships, mines verifying output, farms tracking irrigation, insurers pricing flood risk — all of it now flows from constellations that image the same ground multiple times per day.

The Finnish satellite company in the story captures the same location multiple times daily, which is the property that turns a picture into a measurement. And New York-based Floodbase, which raised funding to turn satellite feeds into flood analytics for parametric insurance, shows the pattern at the other end: don't own sensors, own the transformation from pixels to payout triggers.

What the article adds to the usual enthusiasm is the "asset-light" insight. The most successful new entrants are not launching hardware; they are building detection systems on top of public data, or analytics on top of commercial constellations. The value chain has split: capital-heavy operators below, intelligence companies above.

The better-world case is not abstract. Persistent observation makes externalities visible — the illegal discharge, the unreported deforestation, the emissions nobody declared. Markets and regulators can only manage what they can measure, and measurement from orbit is becoming continuous, cheap and impossible to quietly opt out of. That is the real payload of those thousand satellites.
