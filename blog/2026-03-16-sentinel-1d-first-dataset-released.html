Sentinel-1D First Dataset Released

16 Mar 2026 Source: Sentinel Online EO · Copernicus

Radar is the workhorse of Earth observation precisely because it does not care about weather or darkness, and Europe's radar workhorse just gained a new member. Following the successful launch of Copernicus Sentinel-1D in November 2025 and the publication of its first images, the mission has now released its preliminary dataset — the moment a new satellite stops being a launch story and becomes a data source.

Sentinel-1 carries C-band synthetic aperture radar, which images the surface through clouds, smoke and night. That single property explains why the mission's products underpin flood mapping, ship detection, ground-deformation monitoring for earthquakes and volcanoes, and iceberg tracking — applications where optical sensors are blind exactly when they are needed most. InSAR techniques, comparing radar acquisitions over time, measure millimeter-scale movement of the ground, turning the constellation into a slow-motion seismograph.

The release of preliminary data is an engineering milestone with a precise meaning. Commissioning is the phase where every instrument is calibrated against ground truth: antenna patterns, geometric accuracy, radiometric stability. The published dataset comes with quality disclaimers for good reason — users are invited to look, and to find problems. It is a deliberate act of open peer review at mission scale, and Copernicus's habit of doing it publicly is part of why its data is trusted.

Continuity is the quiet theme. The Sentinel-1 family has already survived the loss of Sentinel-1B in 2021; each new unit secures the revisit times that services downstream have learned to depend on. Every flood map, every deformation alert, every polar track in the coming years will trace back to calibration work like this.

The mundane truth of Earth observation is that the heroics are in the data pipelines. A satellite nobody has heard of publishes a preliminary dataset, and somewhere downstream a hundred operational services quietly keep their promises.
