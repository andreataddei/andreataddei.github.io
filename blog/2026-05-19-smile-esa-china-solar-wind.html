SMILE lifts off: X-raying Earth's magnetic shield

19 May 2026 Source: ESA Space Weather · ESA

SMILE — the Solar wind Magnetosphere Ionosphere Link Explorer — launched on a Vega-C from Kourou and is now heading for one of the strangest orbits in current science: an ellipse that dives to 5,000 km over the South Pole and swings out to 121,000 km over the North. From that vantage point, its wide-field X-ray telescope and ultraviolet imager will watch the global dance between the solar wind and Earth's magnetic shield.

Why it matters

  • First global X-ray view of the magnetopause — the boundary where solar wind pressure reshapes the magnetosphere, seen continuously rather than inferred from single-point measurements.
  • Space weather with a face. Severe solar storms threaten satellites, GPS accuracy and power grids; SMILE provides the imagery that connects cause on the Sun to effect at Earth.
  • A first for ESA-China. The two agencies jointly selected, designed, built and will operate the mission — a working model of cooperation that has become rare.

The orbit buys 45-hour continuous stretches of aurora observation — the first time anyone will watch the auroral ovals evolve unblinking, instead of stitching together snapshots.
