ESA charters its own Crew Dragon mission to the ISS

19 Mar 2026 Source: SpaceNews Space · ESA

At the close of its Council meeting, ESA endorsed a plan to acquire a dedicated SpaceX Crew Dragon mission to the International Space Station, targeted for early 2028 in collaboration with international partners. The mission is conceived as medium-duration — roughly a month — explicitly to maximize Europe's use of the station in its remaining years.

Buying time instead of rides

  • Flight time is the scarce asset. With the ISS horizon finite, Europe's return on investment is measured in astronaut hours for science, maintenance and technology demonstration — not in who owns the rocket.
  • It's a bridge, not a destination. Medium-duration missions build operational experience that feeds directly into Artemis-era ambitions, including lunar surface operations.
  • The asymmetry remains. Europe still launches people on someone else's vehicle — the strategic case for sovereign crew access doesn't disappear, it gets deferred.

The interesting trade: pragmatism now versus capability later. ESA seems to have decided that in the station's final years, an extra month of orbit beats a point of principle.
