Project Aura: The First Android XR Glasses Ship

22 Jun 2026 Source: 36Kr XR · AI

The most advanced smart glasses of 2026 are now shipping, and they arrive with a notable pedigree: the first XR glasses equipped with the Android XR platform and Qualcomm's Snapdragon Reality Elite — hardware that combines full-color displays, spatial anchoring and a top-tier AI assistant in a wearable frame.

The Gemini AI agent is the differentiator, and it is worth pausing on why. A multimodal assistant living on the glasses can see what the user sees, answer about it, and render the answer on a display anchored to the world rather than to the face. That closes the loop that voice-only assistants have always left open: ask about the thing in front of you and get information pinned to the thing in front of you. The underlying silicon — purpose-built XR silicon rather than recycled phone chips — is what makes the loop fast enough to feel like sight rather than search.

The honest caveat in the coverage is the phrase "the iPhone moment has yet to come." The hardware is no longer the constraint; the market-defining application is. Smartphones took off when the App Store made a computer-phone into everything; glasses are waiting for the equivalent — the application that a hundred million people cannot live without, which is not a bigger virtual monitor, however much the enterprise loves that use case.

The betting is that the killer app is AI itself. Assistants are the first software category whose native interface is the field of view: translation, navigation, instruction, memory. A display an inch from the eye is the shortest possible path between an answer and a person.

What ships this year is the platform; what comes next is the profession. Glasses that can see, anchor and answer are a general-purpose instrument — and history says general-purpose instruments get their moment exactly when the first generation of builders treats them as instruments rather than screens.
