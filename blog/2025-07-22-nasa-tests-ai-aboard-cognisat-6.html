NASA Tests AI Aboard CogniSAT-6

22 Jul 2025 Source: NASA EO · AI

The most interesting AI experiment in orbit is not answering questions — it is deciding where to point. NASA is testing AI aboard the CogniSAT-6 cubesat to make Earth-observing satellites smarter about their own observations.

The core problem is scheduling. Today, observation plans are computed on the ground days ahead: which targets, which passes, which sensor settings. But the planet does not wait — clouds roll in, wildfires move, interesting events occur off-plan. A spacecraft that can evaluate its own imagery and retask the next acquisition closes the loop from hours to minutes.

CogniSAT-6, a small flying laboratory with AI accelerators, is NASA's testbed for exactly this autonomy: on-board detection, planning and pointing decisions made in orbit without waiting for a ground station pass.

The engineering challenge is frugality. Radiation-tolerant compute is limited; every model must be compressed and quantized to fit a cubesat's power budget. The spacecraft even tilts tens of degrees off-nadir to aim its imager — an unusual maneuver for a small satellite.

The trajectory is clear and it mirrors the rest of computing: intelligence migrating from the data center toward the sensor. Satellites that decide what is worth looking at are the precursor of constellations that behave less like cameras and more like autonomous observers.

CogniSAT-6 is a commercial-hosted mission as well — built by UK-based Ubotica with partners including ESA — a reminder that the autonomy frontier is being pushed as much by small companies flying experimental hardware as by agencies building flagships. Success here would also validate a procurement model: agencies renting autonomy experiments from small companies instead of building their own cubesats — faster, cheaper, and a market-maker for the NewSpace supply chain.
