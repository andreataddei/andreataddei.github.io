CES 2026: Smart Glasses Take the Stage

5 Jan 2026 Source: ZDNET XR · Wearables

If there was one category that owned the show floor at CES 2026, it was smart glasses. With AI and wearables the two largest trends of the past year, glasses with assistants built in became the focal point of the entire event, and the hardware finally looks like something a normal human would wear.

Two engineering details stand out. First, optics: several models now ship full-color binocular waveguides — a real display in each eye — while weighing under 80 grams, which the category badly needed. Weight is the difference between a demo and a product you forget you are wearing, and waveguide efficiency is what makes it possible. Second, power: one of the highlighted pairs, the Loomos AI glasses, moves the battery into a neckband power bank, claiming up to 40 hours of standby and removing the classic trade-off where every gram of battery sits on your nose.

What makes this moment interesting is not the glasses themselves but what they connect to. An assistant with a camera, a microphone and a display is a sensor platform for AI: it can see what you see, hear what you hear and answer in context. That is the same pattern reshaping Earth observation — sensors everywhere, intelligence layered on top — except here the sensor orbits your head instead of the planet.

The optimistic reading is real, not marketing. Hands-free, glanceable computing is accessibility: it gives information to people whose hands are busy, injured or holding tools. A field technician overlaying a wiring diagram, a surgeon checking a scan mid-procedure, a student translating a lecture in real time. The glasses that left CES 2026 are prototypes no more; they are products on shelves, and the next decade of interface design starts there.
