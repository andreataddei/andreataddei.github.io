Samsung and Google Are Quietly Building AR Glasses

22 Jan 2025 Source: The Verge XR · AI

The most consequential XR news of January was barely a product at all: Samsung and Google confirmed they are developing AR glasses together on the newly announced Android XR platform.

Samsung gives the partnership hardware at scale; Google gives it an operating system and, crucially, Gemini. An assistant that lives on your face and understands what you are looking at is the product everyone from Meta to OpenAI is chasing. Android XR is Google's answer — and glasses are its natural endpoint.

The platform logic matters more than any single device. Headsets like Vision Pro and Galaxy XR are the developer kits; glasses are the consumer product. By shipping the OS first on a headset, Google seeds a software ecosystem — navigation, translation, contextual search — that can later run on hardware light enough to wear all day.

For engineers, the signal is the stack: Android XR brings familiar app development to spatial computing. The skills that transfer — Android tooling, on-device ML for vision and voice — are the ones worth deepening.

Meta demonstrated with Ray-Ban that millions will wear camera-and-audio glasses. The open question of 2025 was whether an ecosystem could beat a vertical product. Samsung and Google placing that bet in public was the strongest answer yet.

Timing is the quiet signal in the announcement. By confirming glasses while the headset was still months from shelves, the alliance told developers which form factor to take seriously — and told Meta that the ecosystem it owns alone today will have a full Android stack for company, sooner rather than later.
