Artemis II: humans round the Moon again, 53 years later

10 Apr 2026 Source: NASA Space · Artemis

Artemis II is home. The ten-day mission launched from Kennedy Space Center on April 1, sent four astronauts around the far side of the Moon, and ended with a Pacific splashdown on April 10. It was the first crewed lunar mission since 1972, and it set records with an almost casual air.

The numbers

  • Farthest humans from Earth, ever: about 406,771 km at peak distance from the planet.
  • Firsts on the crew roster: the first woman (Christina Koch), the first person of color (Victor Glover) and the first non-American (Jeremy Hansen) to fly to the Moon, alongside commander Reid Wiseman.
  • First crewed flight of SLS and Orion — the stack that is supposed to carry Artemis III to lunar orbit, and eventually the surface.
The crew's "Earthset" photograph — Earth dropping below the lunar horizon — was a deliberate echo of Apollo 8's Earthrise in 1968.

Artemis II didn't land anyone; that's Artemis III's job. But it proved the ride works, and it reset expectations for how soon boots return to the surface. The next chapter now has a flight test behind it.
