The Smart Glasses Era Arrives at CES

10 Jan 2025 Source: The Verge XR · AI

Walk the floor of CES 2025 and one category dominated: smart glasses were everywhere. Rokid, Halliday, Xreal, Vuzix and a wave of smaller startups all showed wearables that look like ordinary eyewear but pack micro-displays, cameras and voice assistants.

The striking part was not any single product but the spread of solutions. Some bet on waveguide optics and a tiny green display in one lens. Others pushed audio-first designs with no display at all, betting that a good assistant matters more than a floating image. The industry has clearly converged on the face as the next platform — and just as clearly has not converged on how to build for it.

For an engineer, the interesting data points are the specs: sub-40-gram frames, micro-OLED and waveguide combos getting cheaper year over year, and on-device AI silicon small enough to hide in a temple arm. Every constraint — optics, thermal, battery — is being attacked simultaneously by dozens of teams.

That divergence is healthy. The same chaos produced the smartphone: many wrong answers explored in parallel until the tradeoffs taught everyone what right looks like.

My bet: display-less audio glasses keep growing as the mainstream case, while true AR glasses ride down the cost curve from the top. The years of prototype demos are over; 2025 is when real users start generating real data.

The Verge's reporter came away with the right conclusion: everyone agrees the face is the future, and no one agrees on how to get there. That disagreement is the most exciting engineering brief of the decade — and 2025 is when the market, not the keynote stage, starts grading the answers.
