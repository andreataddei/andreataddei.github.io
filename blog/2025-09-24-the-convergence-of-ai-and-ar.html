The Convergence of AI and AR

24 Sep 2025 Source: Counterpoint Research XR · AI

The industry's research shops spend their lives fitting messy markets into clean categories. Counterpoint's read of Connect 2025 was that the categories themselves had merged: AI and AR are converging into a single product line, and the boundary between them has stopped meaning anything.

The convergence has a technical shape. Augmented reality was always constrained by content: overlaying information on the world required someone to author that information in advance, at scale, in 3D. AI removes the constraint — a model that sees the scene can generate the overlay live, contextual to what is in front of you.

The assistant runs the same trick in reverse: a multimodal model that sees through the glasses' camera does not need the world labeled in advance. Point at a pipe and ask what fitting it takes; look at a menu in a language you do not speak and read it in your own.

What follows is a redefinition of both fields. AR stops meaning floating holograms and starts meaning answers anchored to reality. AI stops meaning a chat window and starts meaning perception.

For product engineers the merged category is a green field: multimodal models, spatial registration, and rendering pipelines that draw directly into a lens. The skills are those of neither classic AR nor classic ML — it is the overlap that is hiring.

Counterpoint's market data underlines the point: glasses shipments now grow in double digits while dedicated AR devices remain niche — evidence that the converged product, not either of its ancestors, is the one consumers are voting for with their wallets.
