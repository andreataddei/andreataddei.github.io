Meta Ray-Ban Display and the Neural Band

17 Sep 2025 Source: AP News XR · AI

The most important interface announcement of the year was not a screen. At Connect 2025 Meta unveiled the Ray-Ban Display glasses and a neural wristband — a band that reads the electrical signals of your hand through the skin, turning subtle finger movements into input for your glasses.

Solve the input problem and you solve the glasses problem. Cameras and microphones gave wearables senses; what they lacked was a way to act. A phone demands your eyes and a hand; voice fails in public; a hand tracking cameras requires. Electromyography — reading muscle activity at the wrist — is the elegant exit: silent, invisible, works with your hands in your pockets.

The display glasses complete the loop: a small high-resolution image in the lens, fed by Meta's AI stack, steered by micro-gestures from the band. It is the architecture of the long-promised consumer AR product, arriving in two parts that each look like an ordinary accessory.

Both devices descend from Meta's Orion prototype, shown a year earlier as a lab feat: displays bright enough for daylight, in a frame light enough to wear. The Orion lesson was that the tech had arrived; Connect 2025 was the industrialization.

For engineers, EMG is the quiet frontier: signal processing and on-device ML turning noisy biosignals into reliable commands. The input device of the next platform is a classification problem — and it just shipped to consumers.

The pricing told the same story: the display glasses landed at $799 with the band included — premium but no longer exotic, firmly in the territory where consumer platforms historically take off. Meta is no longer asking whether the category works; it is pricing for the wave it expects.
